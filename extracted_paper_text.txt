ABSTRACT
Task offloading-based healthcare management in a Cloud-Edge environment emerges as a transformative 
approach that dynamically distributes computational tasks between Internet of Things devices, edge servers, and 
centralized cloud infrastructures. In this research, a Task-Prioritized Distributed Stacked Deep Reinforcement 
Learning strategy is proposed for task offloading-based healthcare management in a Cloud-Edge Environment. 
The physiological data are gathered continuously from the Internet of Things-enabled medical devices in the 
device layer, which are transferred into the edge layer for the operations of preprocessing for enhancing the 
quality of the data. The Hybrid Temporal – Contextual Fusion module is implemented by incorporating the 
Transformer-based attention and Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory networks to capture the temporal de -
pendencies and contextual relationships in physiological signals. A Health Severity Index is analyzed for 
assessing the risk levels of the patient and allows dynamic task prioritization for monitoring the healthcare tasks 
based on the extracted features. An Uncertainty-Aware Priority-Driven Distributed Stacked Deep Reinforcement 
Learning model at the cloud layer resolves the decisions of optimal task offloading between the device, edge, and 
cloud resources. A Stacked Convolutional Neural Network is utilized for learning the system state ’ s compact 
representation, and the Q-values are evaluated for offloading actions. An uncertainty-aware learning mechanism 
and a multi-objective reward function are integrated for enhancing the reliability of the decision. The proposed 
technique is compared to previous mechanisms, attaining a minimum Latency of 170 ms, and a higher resource 
utilization rate of 84 % conforming that the proposed approach substantially enhances energy efficiency and 
overall performance in task offloading-based healthcare management within Cloud-Edge environments.
1. Introduction
In the modern era, healthcare management has become increasingly 
reliant on digital technologies to ensure the timely and effective delivery 
of medical services [ 1 ]. With the exponential growth in the number of 
connected medical devices, including Electronic Health Records (EHRs) 
and real-time patient monitoring systems, the volume of healthcare data 
being generated has surged dramatically [ 2 ]. This rapid digitalization 
demands robust computational infrastructures capable of handling, 
processing, and analyzing healthcare data efficiently. Traditional 
cloud-based solutions often face latency and bandwidth issues, while 
offering vast storage and computing capabilities, particularly when 
real-time decision-making is required [ 3 ]. This has led to the emergence 
of cloud-edge computing paradigms that aim to balance the load be -
tween cloud and edge devices, which offers both the scalability of cloud 
systems and the low-latency responsiveness of edge nodes.
In this context, task offloading becomes a crucial function, where 
healthcare tasks ranging from data collection and pre-processing to 
advanced analytics and diagnostics are intelligently distributed between 
edge and cloud environments [ 4 ]. Effective task offloading ensures 
optimal use of computational resources, reduced response time, and 
improved system efficiency. However, all the tasks in healthcare appli -
cations do not have equal importance; some are time-critical and de -
mand immediate attention, such as emergency response or real-time 
vital sign monitoring, while others, like routine data backups or statis -
tical analyses, tolerate delays [ 5 ]. Therefore, a task prioritization model 
becomes essential to identify and process high-priority healthcare tasks, 
which enhances the overall quality of service and patient care.
Reinforcement Learning (RL) has emerged as a powerful tool for 
optimizing healthcare management by enabling intelligent decision- 
making through continuous interaction with dynamic environments 
[ 6 ]. In healthcare systems, RL is employed to personalize treatment

in cloud-edge environments by learning from historical and contextual 
information to minimize latency and maximize resource efficiency [ 8 ]. 
Unlike traditional rule-based systems, RL offers the advantage of 
self-improvement over time, which makes it particularly suitable for 
complex, data-intensive healthcare scenarios where decisions must 
adapt to changing patient conditions, network states, and system loads 
[ 9 ]. This capability positions RL as a promising approach for creating 
adaptive, responsive, and patient-centric healthcare solutions.
Despite this research, existing offloading strategies often process 
tasks homogeneously, neglecting the critical aspect of task prioritiza -
tion. These methods lead to increased delays for time-sensitive appli -
cations, inefficient resource utilization, and suboptimal patient 
outcomes [ 10 ]. Moreover, many current techniques rely on static 
decision-making processes that do not adapt well to the dynamic and 
heterogeneous nature of healthcare environments. Previous RL-based 
task offloading techniques face limitations such as slow convergence, 
high computational overhead, and inadequate adaptability to dynamic 
healthcare settings. These limitations necessitate a more robust and 
adaptive strategy for task offloading-based healthcare management. To 
address these limitations, the proposed work introduces an optimized 
task-offloading approach tailored for healthcare management within a 
cloud-edge ecosystem. This research proposes a novel Task-Prioritized 
Distributed Stacked Deep Reinforcement Learning (DRL) (DSDRL) 
(TP-DSDRL) approach for intelligent task offloading based on Health -
care Management in Cloud-Edge Environment. The proposed framework 
aims to enhance resource utilization efficiency and minimize energy 
consumption, latency, and computational cost.
1.1. Novelty
The novelty of the proposed TP-DSDRL method in terms of task 
offloading of healthcare information in the cloud-edge computing en -
vironments is presented in this section. 
• A TP-DSDRL framework is proposed for offloading the healthcare 
task intelligently in cloud-edge computing environments. In this 
method, the network conditions, availability of system resources, 
and patient risk are combinely considered to produce the decisions of 
offloading adaptively.
• A Hybrid Temporal – Contextual Fusion (HTCF) method is imple -
mented through the incorporation of Bidirectional Long Short-Term 
Memory (BiLSTM) along with the Transformer-based attention 
mechanism. With this integration, the contextual relationships and 
temporal dependency in physiological signals are captured, resulting 
in enhanced healthcare accuracy in analyzing severity.
• A dynamic task prioritization mechanism is adopted for the health -
care task classification according to the clinical urgency. The phys -
iological indicators, including the status of patient condition, ECG 
abnormalities, and heart rate (HR), are employed for the priority 
score computation, allowing to classify the task into low, medium, 
high, and emergency priority levels.
• For enhancing the decision-making reliability, an Uncertainty-Aware 
Priority-Driven DSDRL (UAP-DSDRL) is integrated to analyze the 
predicted Q values confidence though variance based analysis. Based 
on this mechanism, the increased risk of the actions of offloading is 
prevented during lower prediction confidence.
• A Stacked Convolutional Neural Network (SCNN) is incorporated 
into the architecture of reinforcement learning for processing the 
state information at high dimensional system, such as availability of 
cloud resources, edge workload, network bandwidth, device energy 
level, and task priority.

in Cloud Edge Platform are as follows: 
• A TP-DSDRL framework is proposed for the intelligent task off -
loading in a healthcare system in cloud-edge computing environ -
ments. The network conditions, system resource availability, and 
patient risks are combinely considered for producing the offloading 
decisions adaptively.
• An HTCF module is developed by incorporating the BiLSTM along 
with the Transformer-based attention mechanism, in which the 
contextual relationships and temporal dependencies are captured in 
physiological signals, enhancing the accuracy of analyzing the 
healthcare severity.
• A dynamic task prioritization mechanism is implemented for 
healthcare tasks classification based on clinical urgency. The physi -
ological indicators, including patient condition status, ECG abnor -
malities, and HR, are utilized for priority score computation, 
allowing the classification of tasks into priority levels of emergency, 
high, medium, and low.
• A UAP-DSDRL method is integrated to enhance the decision-making 
reliability and for assessing the predicted Q-values confidence 
through the variance-based evaluation, thereby preventing high-risk 
offloading actions when there is a reduced prediction confidence.
• A SCNN is incorporated in the architecture of reinforcement learning 
for effectively executing the high-dimensional system state infor -
mation, such as device energy level, task priority, edge workload, 
network bandwidth, and availability of cloud resources.

2. Literature review
Recent studies have explored a variety of task offloading approaches 
for healthcare management within cloud-edge platforms. The related 
work on these previous techniques is discussed below.
2.1. Literature related to task offloading for healthcare management in 
cloud-edge platform
Bhasker et al., [ 11 ] presented an efficient augmented moth-flame 
optimization (EA-MFO) approach for managing task offloading in the 
Internet of Medical Things (IoMT) cloud, specifically designed for 
healthcare applications. EA-MFO used a chaos-based logistic map for 
initializing moth positions to ensure high population diversity and better 
exploration of the search space. It optimizes the power consumption and 
makespan with varying cloud resources. Jameil and Al-Raweshidy [ 12 ] 
suggested a Digital Twin Healthcare enhanced asynchronous 
team-based multi-agent proximal policy optimization (DTH-ATB- -
MAPPO) for medical task offloading. The architecture is compatible 
with various communication protocols and relies on partial and binary 
offloading for smooth data transfer. MAPPO learns and adapts to the 
environment dynamically to enhance the quality and resource utiliza -
tion efficiency for decision-making.
Mondal et al., [ 13 ] presented a dynamic energy-efficient offloading 
strategy (DEEO) for task offloading in a mobile edge-cloud computing 
system. In DEEO, a mobile device can offload heavy and secure image 
transfer tasks to the nearest edge server/fog access point to reduce en -
ergy consumption while ensuring the safety and integrity of health data.

Cloud layers. This mechanism led to power savings and decreased 
latency.
Motlagh et al., [ 21 ] have demonstrated a detailed review of the 
predictions of personality traits using AI. They have also mentioned the 
requirements for explainable AI and the benefits of using a combination 
of ML and DL in improving intelligent decision-making systems. Asif 
et al., [ 22 ] have done an exhaustive survey on the applications of ML in 
early disease detection in many medical disciplines like cancer, diabetes, 
and COVID-19.
A Traffic-aware lightweight Hierarchical Offloading framework to -
wards Adaptive Slicing-enabled Space-Air-Ground Integrated Networks 
(SAGIN) was developed by Chen et al., [ 23 ], in which the SAGIN is 
separated into Communication Access Platforms (CAPs) and Computa -
tion Offloading Platforms (COPs). A self-attention-based prediction 
model was implemented accurately to allow adjustments in the slice

based on clipping of proximal along with dynamic confidence intervals 
for optimal offloading. A personalized Federated deep Reinforcement 
learning based computation Offloading and resource Allocation method 
(PFR-OA) was developed by Chen et al., [ 24 ] in which personalized 
demands are considered in smart communities during the proper policy 
generation of resource allocation and computation offloading. A prox -
imal term was implemented to enhance the optimal loss function of local 
Q-values in reinforcement learning.
A Multi-agent Collaborative method for Workflow Task offloading in 
end-edge-cloud environments with the Actor-Critic algorithm named 
MCWT-AC was presented by Xiao et al., [ 25 ], where every MD was 
initially formed as an agent, and the decision of offloading was made 
independently according to the local information. The workflow of 
every MD of the task offloading decision model was acquired using the 
Actor-Critic algorithm. An efficient workflow task offloading plant was 
implemented at runtime using multi-agent collaboration. An Internet of 
Healthcare Things (IoHT) framework was designed by Van Anh and 
Nguyen [ 26 ], incorporating an M/M/C/K priority queue model (M: 
Markovian arrival/service rates, C: servers, K: capacity) along with an 
edge-fog-cloud system of a three-tier architecture. A dynamic priority 
assignment method was designed for the rapid execution of critical 
events using the real-time data of the patient; moreover, an adaptive 
resource allocation method was exploited for performance optimization 
over different workloads.
2.2. Limitations and research gap
The existing work perform important role in increasing computa -
tional efficiency and decreasing latency due to task offloading in cloud- 
edge computing, but it still contains some limitations. The majority of 
existing methods deal with the tasks similarly, without focusing on the 
task priority or application emergency, which causes complex condi -
tions in healthcare where delay-sensitive needs instant processing. 
Moreover, some of the task offloading methods depend on static system 
parameters that include device energy, computational capacity, and 
network bandwidth, but fail to adopt the temporal dynamics and 
contextual relationships in the real-world data streams. Although DRL is 
commonly accepted for adaptive offloading decisions, most of the con -
ventional method has absence of mechanisms to calculate the uncer -
tainty of predicted actions, which causes unpredictable decisions across 
highly dynamic network environments. Another important limitation 
lies in the insufficient representation of high-dimensional system states. 
In many existing models, the ability to extract complex features asso -
ciated with workload fluctuations, resource availability, and network 
congestion remains limited. As a result, these approaches often fail to 
fully capture the intricate relationships that influence offloading de -
cisions. Therefore, a clear research gap exists in the development of 
intelligent, distributed, and priority-aware task offloading frameworks 
capable of effectively modeling temporal and contextual information. 
Such frameworks should also incorporate uncertainty-aware decision 
mechanisms while efficiently handling high-dimensional system states 
to ensure reliable performance for latency-sensitive applications in 
cloud-edge computing environments. To address these issues, this study 
proposes a TP-DSDRL framework. The proposed approach integrates 
priority-based decision making, temporal and contextual learning, and 
uncertainty-aware reinforcement learning to enable efficient task 
offloading.

4. Proposed methodology
A TP-DSDRL framework is proposed for intelligent offloading of 
healthcare tasks in cloud edge computing environments. Three hierar -
chical layer including the device layer, edge layer, and cloud layer is 
contained in this method. The blood pressure, oxygen saturation, heart 
rate, and the ECG signals are the physiological data that are gathered 
continuously from the medical IoT-based devices in the device layer. To 
the edge layer, the gathered data are passed for the execution of pre -
processing operations such as noise removal, normalization, and missing 
value handling to enhance the data quality. These preprocessed data are 
considered through the HTCF module, which incorporates Transformer- 
based attention and BiLSTM networks for capturing temporal de -
pendency and contextual relationships in physiological signals. A 
Healthy Severity Index (HSI) is assessed based on the features extracted 
to analyze the risk level of the patient and allow the prioritization of 
tasks dynamically for monitoring the task based on healthcare. A 
comprehensive state representation is constructed by the system that 
contains the resource availability, network conditions, device energy 
level, and the severity information of healthcare. In the cloud layer, 
optimal task offloading decisions between the cloud resources, edge, and 
device are determined using the UAP-DSDRL model. A SCNN is utilized 
for learning the system state ’ s compact representation, and the Q-values 
are evaluated for offloading actions. An uncertainty-aware learning 
mechanism and a multi-objective reward function are integrated to 
enhance the reliability of decisions during the reduction of operational 
cost, energy consumption, and latency. An adaptive offloading strategy 
is learned by the proposed framework using continuous interaction 
along with the cloud-edge environment, which assures timely execution 
and effective utilization of resources for important healthcare tasks.

4.1. Data acquisition
At the device layer, the processing of data acquisition based on 
healthcare is performed in the proposed TP-DSDRL method. This con -

physiological conditions of the patient continuously. These devices are 
blood pressure monitors, pulse oximeters, heart rate sensors, ECG 
monitors, wearable biosensors, and various smart health tracking sys -
tems. Continuous physiological data streams, including body tempera -
ture, blood pressure (BP), oxygen saturation (SpO2), heart rate (HR), 
and CG signals, are necessary for monitoring real-time health and to 
identify abnormal conditions early.
For the edge layer, the gathered physiological signals are trans -
formed using the wireless communication technologies, including 
cellular networks, Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE), or WiFi. The data 
acquisition process is developed for managing the streaming of data 
continuously during the reliability maintenance and reducing

4.2. Data preprocessing
The incoming raw data based on healthcare encounter systematic 
preprocessing in the edge layer to improve the quality of the signal, 
develop reliable inputs for consequent task prioritization, temporal 
feature learning, and assure data consistency. Continuously, several 
preprocessing operations are executed based on the noise-prone and 
heterogeneous characteristics of physiological signals based on IoT.
Noise Removal: From monitoring devices and wearable devices, the 
physiological signals are gathered, and they are mostly infected using 
sensor instability, environmental interference, and motion artifacts. To 
solve these problems, suitable filtering methods are utilized to eliminate 
the baseline drift and noise at high frequency while maintaining the 
substantial signal patterns clinically. The distorted representation of the 
signal is prevented, which adversely affects the learning performance 
and estimation of downstream severity.
Signal Normalization: The features are scaled into a uniform range 
in normalization since different numerical units and ranges are exhibi -
ted using the acquired healthcare parameters. The numerical stability of 
the model is assured, and the bias is prevented over attributes at high 
magnitude while training the model. The BiLSTM-Transformer model ’ s 
convergence behavior is enhanced through proper normalization, 
resulting in an improvement in the entire efficiency of learning.
Missing Value Handling: Missing samples are contained in the 
healthcare time-series data because of device malfunction, intermittent 
connectivity, or packet loss. Through statistical imputation or 
interpolation methods, the missing values are handled to avoid infor -
mation loss and to preserve temporal continuity, and the input se -
quences ’ structural completeness is assured prior to temporal modeling.
4.3. Temporal and contextual feature learning
The preprocessed physiological series are passed to an HTCF module 
to withdraw the severity-aware and discriminative representations of 
the feature. The self-attention-based contextual modeling is integrated 
along with the bidirectional temporal dependency learning to capture 
the long-range sequential patterns and inter-parameter correlations 
inherent in time series data on healthcare. The multi-head self-attention 
mechanism is utilized to model the contextual dependency between the 
physiological parameters, thereby allowing the clinically substantial 
indicator ’ s adaptive weighting. Following, the attention refined features 
are processed using the BiLSTM for backward and forward temporal 
dependency learning, assuring the anomaly trends and dynamic health

6. Conclusion
This research proposed a novel TP-DSDRL strategy for intelligent 
task offloading in Cloud-Edge healthcare management systems. This 
framework comprises three hierarchical layers such as device, edge, and 
cloud layer, in which the physiological data are gathered in the device 
layer from IoT-enabled medical devices. In the edge layer, the gathered 
data are preprocessed through the preprocessing operations, including 
noise removal, normalization, and missing value handling, which are 
analyzed through the HTCF module by combining the transformer-based 
attention and BiLSTM networks for extracting the features. The HSI is 
assessed for analyzing the risk level of the patients and allows dynamic 
task prioritization. A UAP-DSDRL method is implemented at the cloud 
layer for determining the best decision of task offloading. The SCNN is 
utilized for compact representation learning at the system state and Q 
values estimation for offloading actions. The decision reliability is 
improved through the integration of the uncertainty-aware learning 
mechanism and a multi-objective reward function. In this work, the 
efficiency of the proposed technique was tested by using two healthcare 
datasets. The experimental results showed that the proposed framework 
acquired higher performance outcomes, in which the proposed tech -
nique reduced the latency by 170 ms, the energy consumption of 490 kJ, 
Throughput of 90 Task/sec, Execution time of 620 ms, processing time 
of 0.51 ms, Average task offloading delay of 21 ms, energy efficiency of 
98.03 % and Resource utilization of 84 %. The comparative analysis 
illustrated that the proposed technique outperformed previous methods 
regarding optimized task offloading in Cloud-Edge health care man -
agement systems. The TP-DSDRL method is extended in the future by
